CPE Resources for Insider Threat Practitioners

Every certification worth holding demands continuing education credits, and insider threat practitioners are better positioned than most — the field sits at the intersection of security, behavioral science, law, and investigations, so nearly everything you learn counts somewhere. Better still, hours earned for one credential usually carry to your others: ISC2, for example, automatically applies domain-relevant credits across all your active ISC2 certifications, and most other bodies accept the same certificate of attendance you'd submit anywhere else.

How CPEs work (the two-minute version for new cert-holders)

Most issuers distinguish domain-related credits (ISC2 calls these Group A — activities tied to your certification's subject matter) from general professional development (Group B — leadership, communication, the skills around the skills). The near-universal rate is one credit per hour of qualifying activity. Credits reach your record three ways: some providers submit automatically (give a conference your ISC2 number at registration and the credits appear); some issue a certificate of attendance you self-submit; and for everything else — podcasts, papers, chapter meetings — you claim it yourself with your own documentation and a short summary of what you learned.

Three habits will save you: log activities the week you do them, not the month your cycle ends; keep every certificate in one folder, because issuers audit; and read your issuer's current maintenance handbook once — cycle lengths, annual minimums, and category rules differ by body and change over time (ISC2 · ISACA). Grace periods exist. They are parachutes, not plans.

Where insider threat practitioners earn them

  • NITSIG (National Insider Threat Special Interest Group) — free membership, the largest network of insider threat practitioners, DC-area meetings (JHU-APL Laurel, Herndon), webinars, and the annual Insider Threat Symposium & Expo. The most InT-dense hours you can log.
  • CISA Insider Threat Mitigation — free federal resources, trainings, and self-assessment tools.
  • CMU SEI / CERT — the research house behind much of the field's foundational work; paid courses and certificates for program builders and analysts.
  • CInRM CoE (Canadian Insider Risk Management Centre of Excellence) — practical certificates in insider risk management (screening & risk factors, investigations, organizational integration, OSINT) that ladder into a professional certificate, built with Carleton University's Norman Paterson School of International Affairs (NPSIA). Each practical certificate carries ASIS-recognized CPE (12 credits; aligns with ASIS CPP/PSP/APP).
  • SecureWorld — regional conferences (6–18 CPEs by pass) and free ~1-hour Remote Sessions webcasts with downloadable certificates.
  • GuidePoint GPSEC — free regional one-day conferences with insider threat content; provide your ISC2 number at registration and credits post automatically. The monthly Brick House webinar adds steady hours.
  • Gartner — free research webinars plus the Security & Risk Management and IAM summits. Not an official ISC2 provider: request the certificate of attendance and self-submit.
  • FS-ISAC (financial sector) — and your local ISC2/ISACA chapters; member events and monthly meetings are quiet, steady CPE engines.
  • Give some back — presenting, publishing, and volunteering earn credits at most issuers. Preparing a conference talk counts; so does contributing to community resources like this one.

Tracking

However you track — spreadsheet, folder of PDFs, your issuer's portal — the only system that works is the one you touch weekly. A purpose-built CPE tracking resource for this site is potentially coming soon.

Sources and programs verified July 2026; offerings change — confirm current CPE eligibility with each provider and your certifying body.
